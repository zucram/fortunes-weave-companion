Fortune’s Weave Companion

A small planner and reference for Fire Emblem Fortune’s Weave — not a day-by-day route. You play Eshmel, an avatar sent back from Balor’s ruined future to save four Heroes: the Heroic Games in Dagsion (around 1449), then a timeskip to the war in 1454.

Four things that shape every run

Turns, not clocks

  • One in-game day is four Turns. The clock display is meaningless — time only moves when you do something that costs a Turn, so thinking is free.

One autosave

  • There is a single autosave slot for the whole game. Fortuna’s Blessing (move rewind) is your only safety net — use it early and often.

What carries over

  • Finishing a Hero path carries world-map exploration and completed generic subquests into the next path. Levels, gear and items do not — only into Part 2.

Global progress

  • Support levels, Blessing levels, Notable Deeds and Boons of Salvation are global. Raise them once on any route and every route benefits.

The four Heroes

Pick whichever route you’re playing — the Guide and Recruits tabs both follow it. Eshmel is the solo path against Balor.

    Always useful

    Reference

    Quick answers that apply anywhere in the game.

    Blaze Arts

    • Hero-only abilities that cost HP and the Blaze gauge.
    • Overuse halves max HP for the rest of the battle.
    • Best spent on boss kills or large groups, not chip damage.

    Fortuna's Blessing

    • Rewinds to any earlier point in the battle.
    • Rewind early — cheap fixes beat late panic.
    • If you're rewinding constantly, rethink the plan instead.

    Weapons

    • Combat Arts cost durability — decide in prep, not mid-fight.
    • Enhance at the Shopping Arcade with dungeon materials.
    • Unique weapons need two proficiencies and carry a Curse stat (e.g. Veroquine: B sword + C axe).

    Dungeons & towns

    • Dungeons send a real-time 4-unit squad, then turn-based Clashes.
    • Bank Link Points in Clashes for combo attacks.
    • Dungeon materials fund weapon enhancement; towns have skirmishes and supplies.

    Turns

    • A day is four Turns; time only advances when you spend one. Waiting in menus costs nothing.
    • Weekly Actions at Dagsion are free but refresh once per in-game week: Pale Raven, Shrines, Arena, gifts, meals, stable (Cai only).
    • Leave Dagsion to explore, come back roughly weekly to do your Weekly Actions.

    Recruiting

    • Gifts start a bond; shared meals at inns grow it best.
    • Recruit late in Part 1 and the character arrives at a higher level with better starting gear.
    • Benched units too weak now can be raised to a catch-up method in Part 2.

    Paralogues & missables

    • Nine Paralogues exist, all in Part 1, and each Hero path only sees some of them.
    • They appear for a limited window of in-game days — check the calendar, not just your level.
    • Paralogue completions affect some character availability in Part 3.
    • Nothing is permanently missable: any part of the game can be replayed after the finale.

    Classes

    • Exams have three tiers: Beginner, Specialty (level 10+), Advanced (around level 35 + Renown 8).
    • Exams can fail — check thresholds before spending.
    • Arena training raises proficiencies, including the new Infantry type.
    Part 1 · Recruiting

    Recruits

    Each character needs a Support level with your lord, a Renown level, and usually a quest, item or payment — sometimes a Paralogue too. Requirements differ per route, so this list follows the hero you’re playing. Checkmarks are tracked per route.

    How recruiting works

    Raising the numbers

    • Gifts and shared inn meals raise Supports; theater stage shows do too, once unlocked.
    • Renown rises from main missions, subquests, Paralogues and audiences with the Gods.
    • Supports, Blessing levels and Renown live in a global save — raise them once and every route benefits.
    • Recruiting later in Part 1 brings higher levels and better starting gear.
